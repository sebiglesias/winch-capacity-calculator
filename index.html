Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 1.5x rule for sizing an off-road recovery winch?
The gold standard formula established by off-road recovery trainers is: **Minimum Winch Rating = Gross Vehicle Weight (GVW) × 1.5** A Jeep or truck weighing 6,000 lbs requires at least a 9,000 lb to 10,000 lb rated winch. The extra 50% capacity accounts for thick clay mud suction, steep hill ascents, and power loss on upper rope drum layers.
Why is Synthetic Rope safer than Steel Cable on a recovery winch?
If a steel cable snaps under load, it stores enormous kinetic energy and whips through the air like a lethal steel blade. Synthetic rope stores virtually zero kinetic recoil energy; if it breaks, it simply falls safely to the ground. Synthetic rope is also 80% lighter, floats on water, and doesn't develop razor-sharp wire burrs.
How does a snatch block double a winch's pulling capacity?
Running your winch line through a snatch block pulley anchored to a tree and hooking the line back to your own vehicle bumper creates a 2:1 mechanical advantage. The pulling force is doubled (turning a 10,000 lb winch into a 20,000 lb pulling system) while cutting the electric motor's workload and amp draw in half.
